This note is the working derivation behind Section~V-B of the manuscript. It
answers R1-C1, R2-C3 and Comment~1 of the attached review, which are one
question asked three times: does the FedTSKD convergence bound survive
replacing full participation with SCOPE-FD's deterministic rotation? Facts
1--5 are read directly out of Mu, Garg \& Ratnarajah \cite{mu2024fedtskd}.
Corollaries~\ref{cor:per-client}--\ref{cor:global} compose their Theorem~1
with the manuscript's own Proposition~1. Nothing else is assumed.

Notation follows the manuscript. $E_r$ is the number of local SGD steps in
round $r$, $E_d$ the number of distillation steps per round, $K$ the cohort
size, $N$ the pool size and $R$ the round horizon.

\section{What FedTSKD assumes and proves}
\label{sec:facts}

\textbf{Fact 1.} FedTSKD has no client-selection mechanism. Its Algorithm~1
loops over all $N$ clients every round, and Section~VI states this is by
design: \emph{``Full participation is considered... since the communication
payload required in the FD algorithm is much less than that in the FL
scheme.''} SCOPE-FD's partial-participation setting extends a case the
original paper left open. It does not violate an assumption that paper made.

\textbf{Fact 2.} Assumptions 1--4 of \cite{mu2024fedtskd} are smoothness and
strong convexity of $L_n,Q_n$ (Assumptions 1--2) and bounded-variance and
bounded-gradient conditions on their stochastic gradients (Assumptions 3--4).
Assumption~3's ``uniformly and independently'' clause is about minibatch
sampling $\mathcal{D}_{n,t}\subset\mathcal{D}_n$ \emph{within} client $n$'s
own data, not about which clients are admitted to round $r$. None of the four
references $\mathcal{S}_r$.

\textbf{Fact 3.} The proof in \cite{mu2024fedtskd} (Lemmas~1--2,
Corollaries~1--2, Theorem~1, Appendices B--F) is carried out per client,
tracking a single client's weight sequence $\bm w_{n,t}$ through
$\bm w_{n,t+1} = \bm w_{n,t} - \eta_t \nabla L_n(\bm w_{n,t},\mathcal{D}_{n,t})$
and its distillation analogue. No step depends on any other client. This
follows from Fact~1: with no selection mechanism in the algorithm, there is
nothing selection-related for the proof to condition on.

\textbf{Fact 4.} The time index $t$ ranges over $\mathcal{T}_{LT}\cup
\mathcal{T}_{SD}\cup\mathcal{T}_{LD}$, client $n$'s own elapsed
local-training and distillation steps. Under full participation this
coincides with the round counter, since every client passes through every
phase every round. Under partial participation it does not: a client's $t$
only advances on rounds it is selected. This is the one place a new
statement is needed, not a violated assumption.

\textbf{Fact 5.} The problem statement in \cite{mu2024fedtskd} is $N$
separate per-client minimizations, $\bm w^{*}=\arg\min_{\bm w}L_n(\bm
w),\,\forall n$. The manuscript's additive objective
$F(\{\bm w_n\})=\sum_n\lambda_n L_n(\bm w_n)$ (its Eq.~2) inherits this
directly, so $F^{*}=\sum_n\lambda_n L_n^{*}$ is not a simplification
introduced here.

\section{Given}

\textbf{Proposition 1} (manuscript, already proved): under SCOPE-FD with
$\alpha_u+\alpha_d<1$,
\begin{equation}
\bigl|n_i^{(R)}-n_j^{(R)}\bigr|\le 1 \quad\forall i,j,R.
\label{eq:spread}
\end{equation}
Its exact-count refinement (manuscript Eq.~17), with $m=KR\bmod N$, puts
every client at
\begin{equation}
n_i^{(R)} \;\ge\; \left\lfloor \frac{KR}{N} \right\rfloor \;\ge\; \frac{KR}{N}-1.
\label{eq:lowerbound}
\end{equation}

\textbf{FedTSKD's per-client bound} (\cite{mu2024fedtskd}, Theorem~1):
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\bigl[L_n(\bm w_{n,t+1})\bigr] - L_n^{*} \le \frac{\psi}{2}\cdot\frac{\nu_n}{t+1+\beta_1},
\label{eq:fedtskd-bound}
\end{equation}
with $t$ as in Fact~4 and $\psi,\nu_n,\beta_1$ as defined there.

\section{Why the original Remark understated its case}

The manuscript's earlier Remark (Section~V-B) stated that the FedTSKD bound
``continues to hold... without modification.'' At the level of
\eqref{eq:fedtskd-bound} this is nearly a tautology: by Facts~1--3 the bound
was never conditioned on a selection mechanism, so restating that it still
holds needs no argument. This is R1's objection, and it is correct. The
Remark's analytical content was minimal because it was never really about
partial participation.

The question that does have content is how the bound behaves in
\emph{communication rounds} $R$, not in $t$. Under full participation the two
coincide. Under SCOPE-FD they do not, since $t$ only advances on rounds a
client is selected (Fact~4), and \eqref{eq:fedtskd-bound} alone bounds nothing
in terms of $R$. Proposition~1 is what closes that gap, for every client at
once.

\section{The round-indexed corollaries}

Let $E_{\min}=\min_{r\le R}E_r>0$ and write
$\Gamma_R = (E_{\min}+E_d)\bigl(KR/N-1\bigr)+\beta_1$. Both $E_r$ and $E_d$
are fixed by the training schedule and are independent of the selection.

\begin{corollary}[Per-client rate in rounds]
\label{cor:per-client}
Under Assumptions~1--4 of \cite{mu2024fedtskd} and $\alpha_u+\alpha_d<1$, for
every $R$ large enough that $\Gamma_R>0$, every client $n$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\bigl[L_n(\bm w_n^{(R)})\bigr] - L_n^{*}
\;\le\; \frac{\psi\,\nu_n}{2\,\Gamma_R}
\;=\; \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{N}{KR}\right).
\label{eq:cor-per-client}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Only $n\in\mathcal{S}_r$ update in round $r$, so client $n$'s counter $t$
advances only on rounds it participates in, by $E_r+E_d\ge E_{\min}+E_d$
steps each time. Hence $t_n^{(R)}\ge(E_{\min}+E_d)\,n_n^{(R)}$. Substituting
into \eqref{eq:fedtskd-bound} at $t+1=t_n^{(R)}$ and applying
\eqref{eq:lowerbound}, which holds for every client simultaneously, gives
\eqref{eq:cor-per-client}.
\end{proof}

The word \emph{every} is the content of this corollary. No policy without
Proposition~1's balance guarantee can make the same claim uniformly, since an
in-expectation fairness property alone permits some client's realized
$n_i^{(R)}$ to fall arbitrarily below $KR/N$ at any finite $R$.

\begin{corollary}[Global objective, round-indexed]
\label{cor:global}
Under the same hypotheses, with $\lambda_n=|\mathcal{D}_n|/|\mathcal{D}|$
and $\bar\nu=\sum_n\lambda_n\nu_n$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\bigl[F(\{\bm w_n^{(R)}\})\bigr] - F^{*}
\;\le\; \frac{\psi\,\bar\nu}{2\,\Gamma_R}
\;=\; \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{N}{KR}\right).
\label{eq:cor-global}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$\Gamma_R$ does not depend on $n$, so weighting \eqref{eq:cor-per-client} by
$\lambda_n$ and summing gives \eqref{eq:cor-global}. Use Fact~5 for
$F^{*}=\sum_n\lambda_n L_n^{*}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Consistency check]
\label{rem:consistency}
At $K=N$, \eqref{eq:cor-global} gives $\mathcal{O}(1/R)$, matching FedTSKD's
original rate. This must hold: at full participation SCOPE-FD's selector has
nothing to do, so the bound has to degenerate back to
\cite{mu2024fedtskd}'s own rate.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What is not claimed]
\label{rem:limits}
Corollary~\ref{cor:global} does not beat uniform random selection in order.
Both are $\mathcal{O}(N/(KR))$. The gain of the deterministic rotation is
that the bound holds for each client separately at every finite $R$ rather
than only on average, which is the same distinction Proposition~1 draws for
the participation counts themselves.
\end{remark}

\section{Assumption audit}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=2em]
\item Smoothness and convexity (Assumptions 1--2): properties of $L_n,Q_n$
alone. SCOPE-FD changes no loss function. Unchanged.
\item Bounded variance and gradient (Assumptions 3--4): the same, plus
within-client minibatch sampling, which is unrelated to selecting
$\mathcal{S}_r$. Unchanged.
\item Aggregation: the proof in \cite{mu2024fedtskd} uses $\mathcal{S}_r$
only through the manuscript's aggregation rule (Eq.~5), and SCOPE-FD uses the
identical rule. Unchanged.
\item Sampling scheme: Theorem~1 of \cite{mu2024fedtskd} is proved under full
participation with no selection mechanism and no step referencing any client
but $n$ itself (Facts~1, 3). There is no sampling assumption for a
deterministic rule to satisfy or violate. Corollary~\ref{cor:global} is
therefore an unconditional corollary of Theorem~1 composed with
Proposition~1, not a hedged parallel result.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Status}

Corollaries~\ref{cor:per-client}--\ref{cor:global}, the consistency check and
the assumption audit are now in Section~V-B of the manuscript, and the three
reviewer replies that depended on them are written.

One item remains, and it is confirmatory rather than load-bearing. The script
\texttt{scripts/run\_convergence\_reference.sh} runs $K{=}N{=}30$ plus an
intermediate $K{=}20$ point over five seeds. Once it completes, those points
can be added to the existing $K\in\{1,3,5,10\}$ sweep and the empirical
log-log exponent reported against the $-1$ that
Corollary~\ref{cor:global} predicts. The corollaries hold regardless, given
Assumptions~1--4 and $\alpha_u+\alpha_d<1$.
