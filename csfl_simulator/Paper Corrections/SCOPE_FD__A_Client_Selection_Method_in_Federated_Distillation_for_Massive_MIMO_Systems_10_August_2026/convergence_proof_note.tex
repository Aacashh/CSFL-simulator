This note answers R1-C1, R2-C3, and the attached review's Comment~1, which
are one question asked three times: does FedTSKD's convergence bound survive
replacing full participation with SCOPE-FD's deterministic rotation? Facts
1--3 below are taken directly from Mu, Garg \& Ratnarajah
\cite{mu2024fedtskd}. Corollaries~1--2 compose their Theorem~1 with the
manuscript's own Proposition~1; nothing else is assumed.

\section{What FedTSKD assumes and proves}
\label{sec:facts}

\textbf{Fact 1.} FedTSKD has no client-selection mechanism. Its Algorithm~1
loops over all $N$ clients every round, and Section~VI states this is by
design: \emph{``Full participation is considered... since the communication
payload required in the FD algorithm is much less than that in the FL
scheme.''} SCOPE-FD's partial-participation setting extends a case the
original paper left open; it does not violate an assumption the original
paper made.

\textbf{Fact 2.} Assumptions 1--4 of \cite{mu2024fedtskd} are smoothness and
strong convexity of $L_n,Q_n$ (Assumptions 1--2) and bounded-variance /
bounded-gradient conditions on their stochastic gradients (Assumptions 3--4).
Assumption~3's ``uniformly and independently'' clause is about minibatch
sampling $\mathcal{D}_{n,t}\subset\mathcal{D}_n$ \emph{within} client $n$'s
own data, not about which clients are admitted to round $r$. None of the
four references $\mathcal{S}_r$.

\textbf{Fact 3.} FedTSKD's proof (Lemmas~1--2, Corollaries~1--2, Theorem~1,
their Appendices B--F) is carried out per client, tracking a single client's
weight sequence $\bm w_{n,t}$ through
$\bm w_{n,t+1} = \bm w_{n,t} - \eta_t \nabla L_n(\bm w_{n,t},\mathcal{D}_{n,t})$
and its distillation analogue. No step depends on any other client. This
follows from Fact~1: with no selection mechanism in the algorithm, there is
nothing selection-related for the proof to condition on.

\textbf{Fact 4.} The time index $t$ ranges over $\mathcal{T}_{LT}\cup
\mathcal{T}_{SD}\cup\mathcal{T}_{LD}$, client $n$'s own elapsed
local-training and distillation steps. Under full participation this
coincides with the round counter, since every client passes through every
phase every round. Under partial participation it does not: a client's $t$
only advances on rounds it is selected. This is the one place a new
statement is needed, not a violated assumption.

\textbf{Fact 5.} \cite{mu2024fedtskd}'s own problem statement is $N$
separate per-client minimizations, $\bm w^{*}=\arg\min_{\bm w}L_n(\bm
w),\,\forall n$. The manuscript's additive objective
$F(\{\bm w_n\})=\sum_n\lambda_n L_n(\bm w_n)$ (its Eq.~2) inherits this
directly, so $F^{*}=\sum_n\lambda_n L_n^{*}$ is not a simplification of this
note.

\section{Given}

\textbf{Proposition 1} (manuscript, already proved): under SCOPE-FD with
$\alpha_u+\alpha_d<1$,
\begin{equation}
\bigl|n_i^{(R)}-n_j^{(R)}\bigr|\le 1 \quad\forall i,j,R.
\label{eq:spread}
\end{equation}
Its exact-count refinement (manuscript Eq.~17), with $m=KR\bmod N$, gives
every client
\begin{equation}
n_i^{(R)} \ge \frac{KR}{N}-1.
\label{eq:lowerbound}
\end{equation}

\textbf{FedTSKD's per-client bound} (\cite{mu2024fedtskd}, Theorem~1):
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\bigl[L_n(\bm w_{n,t+1})\bigr] - L_n^{*} \le \frac{\psi}{2}\cdot\frac{\nu_n}{t+1+\beta_1},
\label{eq:fedtskd-bound}
\end{equation}
with $t$ as in Fact~4 and $\psi,\nu_n,\beta_1$ as defined there.

\section{Why the current Remark understates its case}

The manuscript's Remark (Section~V-B) states FedTSKD's bound ``continues to
hold... without modification.'' At the level of \eqref{eq:fedtskd-bound}
this is nearly a tautology: by Facts~1--3, the bound was never conditioned on
a selection mechanism, so restating that it still holds needs no argument.
This is R1's objection: the Remark's analytical content is minimal because
it was never really about partial participation.

The real question is how the bound behaves in \emph{communication rounds}
$R$, not in $t$. Under full participation $t$ and the round count coincide;
under SCOPE-FD they do not, since $t$ only advances on rounds a client is
selected (Fact~4), and \eqref{eq:fedtskd-bound} alone bounds nothing in
terms of $R$. Proposition~1 is what closes that gap, for every client at
once.

\section{The round-indexed corollary}

Let $K_r$ be the local-training step count in round $r$ (fixed by the
training schedule, independent of selection) and $S$ the distillation steps
per round. Let $K_{\min}=\min_{r\le R}K_r>0$.

\begin{corollary}[Per-client rate in rounds]
\label{cor:per-client}
Under Assumptions~1--4 of \cite{mu2024fedtskd} and $\alpha_u+\alpha_d<1$, for
$R$ large enough that the denominator below is positive, every client $n$
satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\bigl[L_n(\bm w_n^{(R)})\bigr] - L_n^{*}
\le \frac{\psi}{2}\cdot\frac{\nu_n}{(K_{\min}+S)\!\left(\dfrac{KR}{N}-1\right)+\beta_1}
= \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{N}{KR}\right).
\label{eq:cor-per-client}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Only $n\in\mathcal{S}_r$ update in round $r$, so $n$'s counter $t$ advances
only on rounds it participates in, by $K_r+S\ge K_{\min}+S$ steps each time.
Hence $t_n^{(R)}\ge(K_{\min}+S)\,n_i^{(R)}$. Substitute into
\eqref{eq:fedtskd-bound} at $t+1=t_n^{(R)}$ and apply \eqref{eq:lowerbound},
which holds for every $i$ simultaneously.
\end{proof}

The word \emph{every} is the content of this corollary: no policy without
Proposition~1's balance guarantee can make the same claim uniformly, since
an in-expectation fairness property alone permits some client's realized
$n_i^{(R)}$ to fall arbitrarily below $KR/N$ at any finite $R$.

\begin{corollary}[Global objective, round-indexed]
\label{cor:global}
Under the same hypotheses, with $\lambda_n=|\mathcal{D}_n|/|\mathcal{D}|$
and $\bar\nu=\sum_n\lambda_n\nu_n$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\bigl[F(\{\bm w_n^{(R)}\})\bigr] - F^{*}
\le \frac{\psi N\bar\nu}{2\left[(K_{\min}+S)(KR-N)+N\beta_1\right]}
= \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{N}{KR}\right).
\label{eq:cor-global}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Weight \eqref{eq:cor-per-client} by $\lambda_n$, sum, and use Fact~5.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Consistency check]
\label{rem:consistency}
At $K=N$, \eqref{eq:cor-global} gives $\mathcal{O}(1/R)$, matching
FedTSKD's original rate. This must hold: at full participation SCOPE-FD's
selector has nothing to do, so the bound has to degenerate back to
\cite{mu2024fedtskd}'s own rate. \texttt{scripts/run\_convergence\_reference.sh}
runs $K{=}N{=}30$ plus an intermediate $K{=}20$ point so the predicted
$N/(KR)$ scaling can be checked against the existing $K\in\{1,3,5,10\}$
points --- confirmatory, not load-bearing: Corollaries~\ref{cor:per-client}--\ref{cor:global}
hold unconditionally given Assumptions~1--4 and $\alpha_u+\alpha_d<1$.
\end{remark}

\section{Assumption audit}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=2em]
\item Smoothness and convexity (Assumptions 1--2): properties of $L_n,Q_n$
alone. SCOPE-FD changes no loss function. Unchanged.
\item Bounded variance and gradient (Assumptions 3--4): same, plus
within-client minibatch sampling, unrelated to selecting $\mathcal{S}_r$.
Unchanged.
\item Aggregation: FedTSKD's proof uses $\mathcal{S}_r$ only through the
manuscript's aggregation rule (Eq.~5); SCOPE-FD uses the identical rule.
Unchanged.
\item Sampling scheme: FedTSKD's Theorem~1 is proved under full
participation with no selection mechanism and no step referencing any
client but $n$ itself (Facts~1, 3). There is no sampling assumption for a
deterministic rule to satisfy or violate. Corollary~\ref{cor:global} is an
unconditional corollary of Theorem~1 composed with Proposition~1, not a
hedged parallel result.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reply text}

\subsection*{R1, Comment 1}
\emph{``...the analytical contribution of Section V-B is minimal.''}

The Remark as written was a statement about a single client's local
trajectory, which does not depend on the selection policy and needs no
argument, as the Reviewer observes -- FedTSKD's Theorem~1 is proved under
full participation with no selection mechanism, so no step of its proof
could have depended on one. The substantive question is different: how the
bound behaves in communication rounds once a client's own update clock and
the round counter diverge under partial participation. We replace the
Remark with Corollary~\ref{cor:global} \todo{cite manuscript number once
placed}, which composes FedTSKD's unmodified bound with our
participation-balance guarantee (Proposition~1) into a rate that holds for
every client, and that recovers FedTSKD's full-participation rate exactly at
$K=N$.

\subsection*{R2, Comment 3}
\emph{``...provide a clearer theorem... or state more modestly that
SCOPE-FD is compatible with the FedTSKD pipeline under the assumptions of
[13].''}

We take the first option, and it did not require falling back to the
second: FedTSKD's Assumptions~1--4 concern only the local loss functions and
within-client minibatch sampling, and its proof is carried out under full
participation with no selection mechanism at all -- there is no sampling
assumption in [13] for SCOPE-FD's rotation to reconcile with.
Corollary~\ref{cor:global} composes the unmodified per-client bound with
Proposition~1 into a round-indexed rate of $\mathcal{O}(N/(KR))$, verified
to recover $\mathcal{O}(1/R)$ at $K=N$ (Remark~\ref{rem:consistency}).

\subsection*{Attached review, Comment 1}
\emph{``...clarify whether the assumptions required in the original
convergence analysis of FedTSKD remain satisfied.''}

Section~\todo{X} now addresses each assumption: smoothness and convexity
concern only $L_n,Q_n$; bounded variance and gradient concern only
within-client minibatch sampling; the aggregation step uses the selected
subset only through the data-size-weighted rule, unchanged by SCOPE-FD.
Most directly: FedTSKD's proof is carried out under full participation and
contains no assumption on how clients are selected, since the original
algorithm has none. There is no sampling assumption to satisfy or violate.
